# Cyclic Group

## 1. 'Cyclic' on Semigroup

Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup, $a\in S$.

With associativity given, let $a^n := \underbrace{a*a*...a}_{n\ \ a}$. 
Obviously, 
$\forall n,m\in N^*, a^{n+m} = \underbrace{a*a*...a}_{n+m\ \ a} = \underbrace{(a*a*...a)}_{n\ \ a}*\underbrace{(a*a*...a)}_{m\ \ a}= a^n*a^m$
$\forall n,m\in N^*, a^{nm} = \underbrace{a*a*...a}_{nm\ \ a} =\underbrace{\underbrace{a*a*...}_{n\ \ a}*\underbrace{a*a*...}_{n\ \ a}*...\underbrace{a*a*...}_{n\ \ a}}_{m \  \ a^n} = (a^n)^m = (a^m)^n$

Let $\mathscr A(a)$ denote $\{a^n \ \ | \ \ n\in N^*\}$. 
Clearly, $(\mathscr A(a), *)$ is a Abelian semigrop. It's naturally cyclic. 
Hence, definition of 'cyclic' is not sufficient on a groupoid, overly sufficient on a group, and just right on a semigroup.

Moreover, it's seemingly isomorphic to positive numbers set equipped with normal addition as $P_a: N^*\rightarrow \mathscr{A}(a)$, $P_a(n) = a^n$ satisfies: 
$\forall n_1,n_2\in N^*,\ \ P_a(n_1+n_2) = P_a(n_1)*P_a(n_2)$, showing that $P_a$ is a homomorphism from $N^*$ to $\mathscr A (a)$. 
However, though $P_a$ is naturally surjective, its injectiveness is not assured here,  hence not sufficiently a homomorphic bijection. 
Unless $P_a$'s injectiveness is assured, $\mathscr{A}(a)$ is not sufficiently isomorphic to positive number sets with normal addition.



Having realized  the lack of sufficiency. Now let's begin to discuss by cases and add conditions to it.

Let's first consider the case where $P_a$ is injective.

According to previous discussion, $\mathscr{A}(a)$ is isomorphic to positive numbers set with normal addition.

**Theorem 1.1**
Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup. $a\in S,\ \ \mathscr{A}(a) = \{a^n \ \ | \ \ n\in N^*\}$.
$P_a$ is injective.
$\longrightarrow $ 
$\mathscr A(a)$ is isomorphic to $N^* = \{1,2,...\}$​.

Injectiveness implies that $\forall n,m \in N^*, a^n\neq a^m$, which somehow shows the essence of positive number system: abstract object with non-repeating progressive operation (normal addition) defined. 
Clearly, $\mathscr A(a)$ here has no identity and therefore is not invertible. Solving equation is not practicable,  just like you cannot solve $x+1 = 0$ for positive number system.



Now let's turn to the case where $P_a$ is not injective. That is, $\exists n,m\in N^*,\ \ (a^n= a^m)\and (n\neq m)$, resulting $\mathscr{A}(a)$​ no more isomorphic to positive numbers with normal addition.

However, without identity and invertible, solving equation is not workable. Hence no more induction is available here. It only shows that all positive numbers divisible by $n$ and $m$ are equal.

## 2. 'Cyclic Subgroup' on Semigroup with identity

Now, let's add identity to $(S,*)$.
Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup with identity $e$. $a\in S$.
To get use of 'identity', suppose $e\in \mathscr{A}(a)\and a^{n_0} = e$.
Clearly, $(\mathscr A(a),*)$​ is a also a semigroup with identity. Moreover, it's abelian and finite. 
No waiting for more discussion, $P_a$ is already not injective here as $a^{n_0} = a^{kn_0} =e$, $k$ is an arbitrary positive number not smaller than $2$.

**Lemma 2.1**
Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup with identity $e$, $a\in S$.
$\exists n\in N^*  ,\ \ a^n = e$ $\longrightarrow$ $\mathscr A(a)$ is a finite set.

Proof:

$(\longrightarrow)$
$\mathscr A(a) = \{a^n \ \ | \ \ n\in N^*\}$. 
Assume $m\in N^*, \ \ a^m = e$. 
$\forall n\in N^* ,\ \ \exists x,y\ \ (x,y\in N)\and (y<n)\and (n = xm+y)$.
$a^n = a^{xm+y} = a^{xm}*a^y = (a^{m})^x*a^y =e*a^y = a^y\in \{a^n \ \ | \ \ n\in J_n\} $

$a$​ has finite order.
Q.E.D.

Note that $(\longleftarrow)$ is not always true as $(S,*)$​ isn't necessarily invertible thus not cancellable. 

**Theorem 2.1**
Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup with identity $e$, $a\in S$.
$\exists n\in N^*  ,\ \ a^n = e$ $\longrightarrow$ $(\mathscr{A}(a),*)$ is a finite group.

Proof:
As $(\mathscr A(a),*)$​ is already finite, associative, Abelian, and has identity, we only prove invertible here.

Suppose $a^{n_0} = e$ and $n$ is arbitrary element in $N^*$.
Case where $n_0 = 1$ is trivial here.

Assume $n_0≥2$.
According to property of modulo operation, $\exists k\in N, \ \  a^n*a^{(n_0-n \% n_0)} = a^{kn_0} =e$.
As $n\%n_0<n$, $n_0 - n\%n_0 ≥ 1$, $\forall a^n \in \mathscr{A}(a), \exists b = a^{(n_0-n\%n_0)}\in \mathscr{A}(a), \ \ a^n*b = e. $​

Hence $\mathscr{A}(a)$​​ is invertible.
Q.E.D.

As a finite Abel group, $\mathscr{A}(a)$​​ is periodic.

**Theorem 2.2**
Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup with identity $e$, $a\in S$.
$($ $\forall n\in N^*, a^n = e$  $\longleftrightarrow$ $\exists k\in N^*,\ \ n = kn_0$ $)$ 
$\Longleftrightarrow$
$n_0$ is the smallest number such that $a^{n_0} = e$
$\Longleftrightarrow$
$(\mathscr{A}(a),*)$ is isomorphic to $(\{C\ \ | \ \ (c^{n_0} = 1)\and(c \in \mathrm C)\} \ \ ,\ \ \cdot)$, $\cdot$ refers normal multiplication of complex numbers. 
$\Longrightarrow$
$\mathscr{A}(a) = \{a,a^2,a^3...a^{n_0}\}$​



Another way to get use of identity without adding $e$ into $\mathscr{A}(a)$ is to intersect $\mathscr{A}(a)$ and $\{e\}$. By this we can assume $P_a$​ a injection without contradictory.

**Theorem 2.3**

Suppose $(S,*)$ is a semigroup with identity $e$, $a\in S$. 
$P_a$ is injective $\rightarrow$ $(\mathscr {A}(a)\cup \{e\} ,*)$ is isomorphic to $(N,+)$. ​

Proof:
Define $a^0 = e$. Add it to $P_a$ to from a new function $P_a': N\rightarrow\mathscr A(a)\cup \{e\} $, which is obviously a bijective homomorphism.

Hence,  $(\mathscr {A}(a)\cup \{e\} ,*)$ is isomorphic to $(N,+)$. 
Q.E.D.



Additionally, $(\mathscr {A}(a)\cup \{e\} ,*)$ is also isomorphic to non-positive numbers with normal addition.

## 3. 'Cyclic Subgroup' on Group

**Theorem 3.1**
Suppose $(G,* )$ is a group. $a,a^{-1}\in G$. $n_0$ is an arbitrary positive number. 
$a^{n_0} = e$ $\longleftrightarrow$ $(a^{-1})^{n_0} = e$ $\longleftrightarrow$ $\forall k(k\in N^*)\and(1\leq k\leq n_0),\ \ a^k = (a^{-1})^{n_0-k} $.

Tips of Proof:
When $a^{n_0} = e$, $\mathscr{A}(a)$ is a finite group, which is cancellable.

**Theorem 3.2**
Suppose $(G,* )$ is a group. $a,a^{-1}\in G$.
$(\mathscr A(a),*)$ is isomorphic to $(\mathscr{A}(a^{-1}),*)$. 

**Theorem 3.3**
Suppose $(G,* )$ is a group whose identity is $e$. $a,a^{-1}\in G$.
$P_a$ is injective $\longleftrightarrow$ $P_{a^{-1}}$ is injective 
$\longleftrightarrow$
$\mathscr{A}(a), \mathscr{A}(a^{-1})$ are untwisted semigroups.
$\longleftrightarrow$ 
$(\ \ \mathscr{A}(a) \cup \{e \} \cup \mathscr{A}(a^{-1}),*\ \ )$ is isomorphic to $(Z,+)$​​.



In summary, as for a semigroup, repeatedly operating one element with itself generate a isomorphism to $(N^*,+)$ iff $\forall n,m,\ \ a^n\neq a^m$. 
If you want a isomorphic to $\{1,2,... N\}$, just take a n-oder segment from $\mathscr A(a)$.

In a semigroup with identity, $ a^n\neq a^m$ implies identity $e$ is not in $\mathscr A(a) = \{a,a^2,a^3...,\}$. 
$(\mathscr {A}(a)\cup\{ e\},*)$​​​ is isomorphic to natural numbers with normal addition.

Similarly, in a group where  $\forall n,m ,\ \ a^n\neq a^m$, 
$(\ \ \mathscr{A}(a) \cup \{e \} \cup \mathscr{A}(a^{-1}),*\ \ )$ is isomorphic to $(Z,+)$​​.

Cyclic semigroups above are all infinite. In case of semigroup with identity, if $e$ is in $\mathscr{A}(a)$, it becomes a finite group isomorphic to $\{c\ \ | \ \ (c^{n_0} = 1)\and(c \in \mathrm C)\}$ with normal multiplication of complex numbers.
