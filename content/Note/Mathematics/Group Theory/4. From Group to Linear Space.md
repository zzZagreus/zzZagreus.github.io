# From Group to Linear Space

**Definition 1:**
Suppose $S$ is a nonempty set and $*$ and $\cdot$ are binary operations on $S$.
$(S,*)$ is a semigroup and $(S,\cdot)$ is a group 
$\and$  
$\forall x\in S$ $x$'s left and right transitions under $*$ are endormorphisms of $(S,\cdot)$
$\Longleftrightarrow$
 $(S,*,\cdot)$ is a ring.

**Definition 2:**
Suppose $(S,*,\cdot)$ is a ring.
$(S,*)$ is a commutative group $\Longleftrightarrow$ $(S,*,\cdot)$​ is a field.

**Theorem** **2.1**
Suppose $(A,*)$, $(B,\cdot)$ are groups with identity $e_A$ and $e_B$.
$f$ is a homomorphism from $A$ to $B$ $\longrightarrow$ $f(e_A) = e_B$.

**Definition 3:**
Suppose $(V,*)$ is a group and $(F,+,\times)$ is a field.
$\exists f:F\times V\rightarrow V$​ such that
1 ）$\forall x\in F,f_x:V\rightarrow V $ is a endormorphism on $(V,*)$.
2 )  $\forall v\in V, f_v:F\rightarrow V$ is a homomorphism from $(F,+)$ to $(V,*)$
3 ）$\forall x,y\in F, v\in V, f[(x\times y),v] = f[(x,f(y,v)]$
$\Longleftrightarrow $
$(V,* ,(F,+,\times))$​​ is like a linear space.

You may notice that we don't give the property of 'identities' in $f$. It's because they are sufficient with positions above:

Suppose $e$, $I$ are identities of $(F,+),(V,*)$.

$\forall x\in F,f(x,I) = f(x,I*I) = f(x,I)*f(x,I)$. 
That is $f(x,I) = f(x,I)*f(x,I)$. Operate $f(x,I)$'s inverse whose existence is clear with both sides. $f(x,I) = I$.

$\forall v\in V, f(e,v) = f(e+e,v) =f(e,v)*f(e,v)$.
By the same token $f(e,v) = I$​.
(You can also finish proof above quickly with theorem 2.1)

Suppose $e_\times$ refers to the identity of $(F,\times)$.
Position 3) shows that if $f_{e_\times}$ is an injection, then $\forall v\in V, f(e_\times,v) = v$。
